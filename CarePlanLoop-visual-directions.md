# CarePlanLoop — visual directions

**For review before identity design**  
**Reference reviewed:** [JoinSpread](https://joinspread.app/?ref=saaspo.com)  
**Date:** 20 October 2026  
**Status:** Three distinct visual territories; no direction selected and no logo/assets created yet.

## Reference notes and non-negotiable typography rule

JoinSpread's current homepage uses a compact pill-shaped navigation, quiet supporting copy, large image-led space, a cinematic opening, offset image panels, rounded media, and a shift into a warm light section. The hero headline is much larger than CarePlanLoop should use. The useful cues are the pacing, generous breathing room, restrained UI details, and contrast between focused imagery and clean content—not the oversized hero type, resale imagery, or Spread's identity.

**CarePlanLoop type rule across all directions:** no oversized, bulky, extra-bold headlines. Aim for a calm editorial scale: homepage H1 around **42–50px desktop / 32–38px mobile**, weight **400–500**; section headings around **28–36px**, weight **400–500**; body **16–18px**; interface labels **12–14px**. Use width, spacing, line breaks, and contrast for hierarchy instead of heavy weight. These are starting ranges for design exploration, not locked tokens.

The reference is for visual language. The scroll-story references (Lux Capital, Blackbird, and Klimori) remain interaction references only.

## Direction 1 — Care Ledger

**Character:** quiet, precise, trustworthy, human-accountable.  
**Core metaphor:** one month of care work as a living ledger of evidence and handoffs.

- **Composition:** a split editorial opening with a modest statement at left and a clear, custom-drawn month timeline at right. As chapters progress, the same timeline gains events and reviewer notes. Use full-width transitions between the timeline, a focused exception view, and a separate billing-evidence lane.
- **Palette:** warm white, soft paper, deep charcoal, muted botanical green, and a restrained clay/amber exception accent. Keep status colors distinct and never rely on color alone.
- **Typography:** humanist sans for headings and body; compact mono or tabular numerals only for timestamps, source IDs, and workflow labels. Quiet scale, regular/medium weights.
- **Illustration/UI:** original vector timeline, source slips, owner labels, fine dividers, review marks, and source-to-event connectors. No stock patient portraits, medical cross cliché, EHR imitation, or decorative dashboard charts.
- **Shape and surface:** square-cornered editorial panels with a few soft-radius evidence windows; fine rules instead of heavy card shadows. Keep the pill CTA/nav details subtle.
- **Motion cue:** the timeline accumulates a consent event, task, report, human review, and separate staff-time entry. Each change shows a real state transition; an unresolved exception remains open until the fictional human sign-off.
- **Best fit:** strongest default for the brief because it makes provenance, ownership, and billing separation legible without claiming a clinical result.

## Direction 2 — Open Handoff

**Character:** contemporary, cinematic, warm, approachable.  
**Core metaphor:** an open loop of work moves between people until a named reviewer closes the handoff.

- **Composition:** a calm deep-toned opening with a compact headline and a dimensional, abstract timeline object; scroll shifts into pale neutral chapters with asymmetric, floating workflow panels. Use a few large visual moments, then quieter explanatory sections. It borrows JoinSpread's contrast and spacious image rhythm, not its photo subject or oversized type.
- **Palette:** deep forest or ink for the opening; warm ivory and light stone for reading sections; muted copper for pending exceptions; calm green only for completed human review.
- **Typography:** understated neo-grotesk, regular weight, short lines, and modest headlines. Reserve caps/mono for chapter markers and metadata. Avoid display-heavy or shouty type.
- **Illustration/UI:** abstract paper-like layers and fine-line workflow annotations that resolve into the CarePlanLoop timeline. No human/clinical stock photography; use original diagrams and synthetic product UI instead.
- **Shape and surface:** generous negative space, a few offset rounded windows, deliberate edge crops, and soft depth. Do not turn every content item into a floating card.
- **Motion cue:** the visual focus follows a reported exception to its assigned owner, then pauses in a review state. Nothing auto-closes or implies a clinical recommendation.
- **Best fit:** if the user wants the homepage to feel most cinematic and memorable while keeping product screens secondary to the story.

## Direction 3 — Clinical Field Notes

**Character:** observant, editorial, evidence-led, quietly technical.  
**Core metaphor:** a team's working record, annotated over a month by different accountable roles.

- **Composition:** a structured page with a narrow date/role rail, a broad narrative column, and marginal source notes. Chapters read like successive entries in a case-free operational journal. The demo becomes a carefully typeset annotated record, not a generic dashboard.
- **Palette:** chalk, warm grey, graphite, desaturated sage, and a limited muted rust for unresolved items. Use paper contrast and rules for organization; avoid sterile hospital blue.
- **Typography:** restrained text serif or soft humanist sans for editorial sentences; neutral sans for UI; small mono for IDs, dates, and evidence tags. Keep all headline sizes moderate and weights regular.
- **Illustration/UI:** line-based timeline, margin annotations, dated source tags, reviewer stamps, and explicit “reported / routed / reviewed / billing review” labels. No pseudo-medical graphs, heartbeat lines, or faux EHR elements.
- **Shape and surface:** almost no conventional cards; use rules, indentation, marginalia, and changes in column width to differentiate content. Slightly tactile paper texture only if it does not reduce contrast or readability.
- **Motion cue:** notes and evidence appear in chronological order; source references connect to their event; an unresolved entry stays visibly marked. Reduced-motion view presents the complete annotated record.
- **Best fit:** if trust and detailed scrutiny from clinical, compliance, and RCM reviewers are the leading priority.

## Shared system requirements whichever direction is chosen

- Preserve CarePlanLoop's original identity; JoinSpread is a reference, not a template.
- Make the site feel light and premium, but allow a dark opening if chosen territory uses it; keep reading sections comfortable and uncluttered.
- Use native scrolling. A visible skip path and complete still-state story are required. Honor reduced-motion settings, keyboard use, mobile stacking, and focus visibility.
- Show “Concept preview · synthetic data” anywhere the product demo appears. Keep owner, source, timestamp, review state, unresolved/abstained status, and separate billing evidence visible.
- Keep the five source offers distinct. Avoid interchangeable feature cards, fake outcome counters, testimonials, customer logos, integration badges, or product claims not supported by the brief.
- Before high-fidelity pages: confirm the selected territory, then create the CarePlanLoop wordmark/logo, favicon, palette, typography tokens, icon/illustration rules, and motion system.

## Suggested selection

**Recommended: Care Ledger.** It is the clearest match for the buyer's need to inspect a handoff and its evidence. It can still borrow JoinSpread's generous spacing, restrained UI, and image-like transitions, while keeping typography modest and maintaining a visible, credible product workflow.
