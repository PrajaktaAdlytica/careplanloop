Workflow problem / research hypothesis

The handoff between events is the question.

Enrollment, consent, care-plan tasks, RPM reports, follow-up, and billing evidence may be spread across different people and tools. We need to learn where this actually creates work for each organization.

01Possible handoff gaps

Four separate moments. One question of ownership.

01
Enrollment and consent

Eligibility review and consent evidence may be separated from the care-team work that follows. The source, notice version, and authorized reviewer need to be clear.

Hypothesis · validate by role
02
Longitudinal tasks

A clinician-approved task can move between care managers, nurses, and teams. The next owner and the evidence of follow-through can become difficult to see.

Hypothesis · validate by role
03
Reported RPM events

A device report may need context and human review. A reading should stay distinct from staff action, clinical sign-off, and any treatment decision.

Hypothesis · validate by role
04
Time and billing review

Staff contact and time records have to be reconciled by qualified billing owners. An automated touch or device report does not itself establish billability.

Hypothesis · validate by role
02What to test

Open discovery questions

First understand the current system.

How do teams handle consent, eligibility review, care-plan assignments, device-report exceptions, and staff-time documentation today? Which parts already work well in the EHR, outsourced workflow, or RPM platform?

Clinical operations and RCM should be interviewed separately. Test alert fatigue, device coverage and rights, staffing capacity, overlapping programs, payer variation, permission to access records, and measurable net workload.

03The first test

Study one governed workflow before building into it.

A retrospective workflow replay with an appropriately consented cohort can compare documentation completeness and response time with a stated baseline.

Agree in advance on a denominator, local SLA, nurse-minute guardrail, missed-escalation guardrail, device-feed rights, and explicit escalation stop rule. A synthetic demo cannot establish outcomes, billability, reimbursement, or ROI.
